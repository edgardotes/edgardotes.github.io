**Welcome!**
I’m Edgar Dolores-Tesillos, a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Universität Bern, specializing in atmospheric science. My work delves into the intricate dynamics of large-scale atmospheric processes and their far-reaching impacts.

I completed my PhD at the Institute of Meteorology at the Free University of Berlin, where I honed my skills in analyzing atmospheric circulation patterns and managing large ensembles of climate models. My research explored how climate warming influences the structure and behavior of intense extratropical cyclones. Through extensive climate data analysis and programming in languages like Fortran, MATLAB, and Python, I uncovered key findings:

Future extratropical cyclones are likely to have a broader footprint of strong winds, driven by increased moisture in a warming climate.
These insights emphasize the importance of accurately representing moist processes, such as cloud formation, in climate models.
The emergence of kilometer-scale climate simulations offers exciting possibilities, as higher-resolution models promise improved simulations of both large-scale circulation and crucial moist processes.

I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Mexico, which laid the foundation for my passion for atmospheric science.

On this site, I share updates on my research and current projects, which focus on:

Extratropical cyclones and their evolving dynamics.
Atmospheric blocking and its implications.
Impacts of atmospheric processes on renewable energy production.
Feel free to explore, and thank you for visiting!
